Website Lab

Is this business doing its website right, and does it even matter for their sales? Find the mistakes on three made-up businesses, then grade real websites with one report card.

How the activity works

About 10 minutes per round. Works in small groups with one laptop or phone each.

  1. Open the bad siteGive groups 3–4 minutes to find as many mistakes as they can. There are 12 hidden on each site.
  2. Grade itScore it on the report card below, then rate how much the website drives the business's sales. Compare between groups.
  3. Reveal the answersPress “Show the 12 mistakes” in the bar at the bottom of the site. Which group found the most?
  4. See the fixOpen the fixed version and talk about which fix would help the business most.

Presenting? On any demo site, press R to show or hide the answers, and use “Hide bar” to give people a clean view.

Running it as a competition? Give teams the Website Hunt link instead (it’s public, so no sign-in). It has the same three bad websites plus a list for writing down mistakes, but no answers and no fixed versions.

The report card

Eight questions a customer asks without realizing it. Score each one 0 (missing), 1 (partly) or 2 (nailed it). Then rate the website's impact: how much does it actually make the business money? A bad grade only hurts as much as the website drives sales. Grades you give are saved in this browser only.

Real websites to grade

A mix of famous bad sites, sites that break the rules on purpose, ugly sites that make a lot of money, and sites worth copying. The last two groups make the best debates.

Breaks the rules

Classic examples of what not to do.

Arngren

Retail
arngren.net

A Norwegian electronics and gadget store that has shown up on “worst website” lists for years.

Look for: hundreds of product photos crammed onto one page, mixed fonts and colors, no clear way to navigate.

Website impact: High (likely). It’s a store that sells online, so the homepage is its shop window.

Ask the roomIf you wanted to buy one scooter, how long would it take you to find it?

Penny Juice

Food & drink
pennyjuice.com

A drink supplier for schools, often cited for a busy rainbow of colors and hard-to-find navigation.

Look for: color overload, how you get from page to page, and whether a school buyer can find what to order.

Website impact: Medium. School buyers check the site to learn about the products and how to order.

Ask the roomWho is this site for: kids, parents or school buyers? Does the design match?

User Inyerface

Game
userinyerface.com

A sign-up form made frustrating on purpose by the design agency Bagaar, as a lesson in bad UI.

Look for: tricky buttons, confusing checkboxes, a pop-up that keeps coming back. Race to finish it.

Website impact: Not a business. It’s a game, and also an ad for the agency that made it.

Ask the roomWhich of these tricks have you seen on a real website?

Breaks the rules on purpose

Bad by the checklist. Is it bad for business?

LINGsCARS

Debate
lingscars.com

A UK car-leasing company famous for animated GIFs, clashing colors and chaos. Founder Ling Valentine built it that way on purpose, and the business has been widely reported as successful.

Look for: what's underneath the chaos. Can you still find a car, a price and a way to apply?

Website impact: High. It leases cars online with no showroom, so the website is the whole store.

Ask the roomIs a memorable personality worth breaking every design rule?

Yale School of Art

School
art.yale.edu

The art school's site works like a wiki that its community can edit, so it has looked strange on purpose for years.

Look for: whether an applicant can still find deadlines and how to apply.

Website impact: Medium. Applicants use it to research the school and find deadlines.

Ask the roomWhen does an unusual site fit the brand, and when does it just get in the way?

Ugly, but it works

Plain sites from very successful companies. Grade them, then ask how much the website matters to their revenue.

Berkshire Hathaway

Debate
berkshirehathaway.com

Warren Buffett's company: one of the most valuable in the world, with a homepage that is a plain list of links on a white page. GEICO, the Berkshire business that sells to consumers, has its own modern website.

Look for: how fast you can find the shareholder letters. Then try it on your phone.

Website impact: Low. Its revenue (about $371 billion in 2025) comes from businesses it owns, like GEICO and BNSF Railway. Nobody buys anything on this website.

Ask the roomIt gets a bad grade. Does that cost Berkshire any money? Would a redesign be worth it?

Craigslist

Marketplace
craigslist.org

Classified ads that have barely changed in decades. Almost no images, no animations, just links.

Look for: how quickly you can find, for example, a used bike near you.

Website impact: High. The website is the whole business.

Ask the roomWould a prettier site help Craigslist, or confuse its loyal users?

Worth copying

Clear, fast and built around what visitors came to do.

GOV.UK

Government
gov.uk

The UK government's website won Design of the Year in 2013. Its published design principles start with “Start with user needs” and “Do less”.

Look for: plain language, a big search box, nothing decorative.

Website impact: High. Not about revenue: millions of people rely on it to get government tasks done.

Ask the roomWhat could a small business learn from a government website?

Airbnb

Travel
airbnb.com

The homepage leads with the one thing visitors came to do: search for a place to stay.

Look for: the search bar (where, when, who) and how little else competes with it.

Website impact: High. Bookings happen on the website and app.

Ask the roomWhat is the “one thing” visitors come to do on a bakery's website?

Duolingo

Education
duolingo.com

One short sentence about what it is, one big button to get started, and a friendly brand that's consistent everywhere.

Look for: how many words it takes them to explain the product.

Website impact: High. The website and app are the product.

Ask the roomWrite a one-sentence headline like theirs for a business you know.

Bring your own

Usually the most useful round.

Have each group pick a real local business near school (a pizza place, a dentist, a car wash) and grade its website with the report card. Pick the one fix that would help it most. If a club wanted to offer free website check-ups to local businesses, this is the checklist to use.

Websites change. Open each link before the meeting to make sure it still shows what's described here.
