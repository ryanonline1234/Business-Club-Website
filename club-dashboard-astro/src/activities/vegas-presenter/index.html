Vegas on a Budget

Your first job on the Strip pays $3,600 a month. Budget it, survive the Wheel of Life, bet on what you know, and find out why the house always wins (and how you can be the house).

The game plan

Every team gets the same job and the same paycheck. The team with the highest net worth at the end wins: emergency fund, plus investments, plus chips, minus credit card debt.

  1. 3 minDeal inForm teams. One phone per team opens the team sheet and enters a team name.

  3. 10 min2 · Wheel of LifeFast-forward six months, then spin 3 or 4 times. Surprises hit every team.
  4. 12 min3 · Over/UnderEight money questions. Teams bet chips on what they know.
  5. 8 minBe the houseGambling vs. investing, then teams read out their net worth.

Round 1 · 12 minutes

Build the budget

Each team picks one option per category on the team sheet. Cheaper options leave more to save; nicer ones earn stars.

At least ★Living on rice and the bus is cheap, but nobody sticks with it. A budget has to be one you can live with.
50 / 30 / 20Aim for about 50% on needs, 30% on wants, 20% saved. The cheapest option in a category is the need; paying more for it is a want.
Split your savingsChoose how much goes to an emergency fund (cash for surprises) vs. investments (they grow, but they're locked away).
Talking points
  • Nevada has no state income tax, so a $52,000 job takes home about $3,600 a month here. In many states it'd be less.
  • Rent is the big one. A common rule: keep housing near 30% of take-home pay. That's about $1,080 here.
  • Spend more than you make and the gap goes on a 24% credit card. Watch the "Left over" number.
Round 2 · 10 minutes

The Wheel of Life

Tell teams their budget has run for six months; the team sheet shows what they've saved (or owe). Spin 3 or 4 times. After each spin, every team taps the same event on their sheet.

LIFE

Ready when you are

Spin the wheel to see what life throws at everyone.

Talking points
  • Surprises aren't rare: something like this happens to most people every year. That's what an emergency fund is for. Aim for 3 to 6 months of expenses.
  • No emergency fund? The surprise goes on a credit card, and it costs you 24% a year until you pay it off.
  • On optional spins, ask teams how they decided. Saying no is a money skill too.
Round 3 · 12 minutes

Over / Under

Each team starts with in chips. Read the question and the line. Teams pick over or under, bet any amount, and lock it. Then reveal.

The line
Finale · 8 minutes

The house always wins. So be the house.

Casinos don't need luck. Every game is built so they keep a small slice of every bet, and millions of bets turn that slice into billions.

5.26¢kept from every $1 bet on American roulette, on average. Thirty-eight pockets, but wins pay as if there were 36.
7–9¢kept from every $1 played in Strip slot machines, year after year. Replay your winnings and the slice comes out again.
~$9 billionwon from gamblers by Las Vegas Strip casinos in a single year. The edge is small; the volume is huge.

Talking points
  • A broad index fund owns a slice of hundreds of companies, casino companies included. Over the long run the edge is on your side, so you get paid for waiting.
  • The stock market has averaged about 10% a year before inflation, with some ugly years (−37% in 2008). We use 7% to be careful.
  • The biggest lever is time. Starting at 22 instead of 32 roughly doubles what you end up with.
